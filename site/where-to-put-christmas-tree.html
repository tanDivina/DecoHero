Christmas tree placement

Where to put your Christmas tree

The best spot is close to an outlet, at least 3 ft from heat, out of the walkway and in view from where you sit. In a small living room that usually means a corner, the side of a window, or the spot of a chair you move out for December.

Before you pick a spot

  • An outlet nearby. Lights need power, and a cable across the floor is a trip hazard. Run a short extension along the wall, not across a walkway.
  • 3 ft from heat. The NFPA, the US fire protection association, advises at least 3 ft between the tree and any heat source: fireplaces, radiators, heat vents, space heaters, candles and lamps that run hot.
  • Doors and walkways stay clear. A full tree is about two thirds as wide as it is tall. Check that every door still opens all the way.
  • Visible from the sofa. You will look at the tree most from your seat, so check the view from there, not from the doorway.

A corner

A corner takes up the least usable floor, hides the cable, and leaves the room's main paths alone. It also suits homes with toddlers or pets, because there are only two open sides to guard. If the corner is next to a door, use another corner: a tree that blocks a door swing gets knocked every time someone comes in.

Next to a window, or in front of it

A tree in a window is seen from the street. The trade-offs are less daylight, curtains you can't close, and in many homes a radiator under the window, which is too close to a real tree. Standing the tree beside the window instead keeps most of the light and still shows the lights from outside.

In a small living room, swap out a piece of furniture

In a small room the easiest floor to find is under something you can live without for a month: an armchair, a side table, a plant stand, a stack of extra chairs. Move it to a bedroom or storage and give the tree its spot. In the sample below the tree stands where a stack of plastic chairs used to be, and the rest of the room is unchanged.

Other options when floor space is short: a slim or pencil tree, which needs about half the floor of a full fir, or a 3 to 4 ft tabletop tree on a side table or a console.

In an open floor plan

Put the tree against a wall or a large piece of furniture that faces the seating area, so it belongs to the living room and not to the kitchen or the hall. Keep it out of the line between the kitchen and the sofa, which is the path people walk most.

Next to the TV

A tree beside the TV wall is in view whenever you sit down, and the outlets are already there. Leave enough space that the branches do not cover the screen or the speakers, and keep it away from the back of electronics that run warm.

Questions

Common questions

Where should a Christmas tree go in a small living room?

In a corner, beside a window, or in the spot of a chair or side table that you move out for December. Keep it 3 ft from heat and out of walkways.

Can I put a Christmas tree in front of a window?

Yes, and it is seen from outside. Check for a radiator or heat vent under the window first: the NFPA advises 3 ft between the tree and any heat source.

How far should a Christmas tree be from a fireplace or radiator?

At least 3 ft, according to the US National Fire Protection Association. Water a real tree every day as well.

Where do you put a Christmas tree in an open floor plan?

Against a wall or a large piece of furniture that faces the seating area, out of the path between the kitchen and the sofa.
